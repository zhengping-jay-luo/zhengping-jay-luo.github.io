Explore Your Professional Identity

This is a program to support you explore a topic that is personally meaningful and connected to your academic interests, professional goals, or intellectual curiosity. The purpose is to help you develop and communicate your emerging academic and professional identity while applying the knowledge, research practices, and critical-thinking skills developed in this course. Your project should answer three central questions:

  • What did you explore, create, or experience?
  • Why is it meaningful to you?
  • How does it connect to your academic development, professional goals, or future interests?
You are not expected to begin as an expert. Thoughtful curiosity, meaningful investigation, and evidence of learning are more important than choosing an advanced topic. You may deliver your presentation using slides, a poster, a live demonstration, or any another format of your choice. You may select a topic and format that align with your interests. Possible approaches include: -
  • Investigating an emerging technology, research area, or professional issue.
  • Sharing and critically reflecting on a meaningful academic or professional experience.
  • Creating a program, prototype, model, visualization, or demonstration to explore a problem or concept.
  • Applying a course concept to a real-world problem
  • Exploring a career path and the knowledge or skills needed to enter it
  • Presenting a distinctive perspective on a topic connected to your goals

Projects may take the form of an investigation, creative work, technical artifact, personal-professional reflection, or a combination of these. A project does not need to produce a successful program or polished invention. A carefully documented attempt that identifies challenges, analyzes unexpected results, and demonstrates meaningful learning can be highly successful.

Evaluation Criteria

When you're ready, you will be asked to give a presentation on your chosen topic. The project will be evaluated on evidence of meaningful exploration. The following criteria will be used to evaluate your presentation:

  • Content and Understanding (50%): Presents accurate, relevant, and well-researched information; explains important ideas with appropriate depth; acknowledges credible sources; and goes beyond summary by applying ideas, making connections, evaluating evidence, solving a problem, creating something, or offering a thoughtful interpretation.
  • Personal and Professional Connection (30%): Clearly explains why the topic matters to the student and how it connects to academic interests, professional goals, experiences, or future development.
  • Organization and Communication (20%): Communicates a clear central purpose, follows a logical structure, and presents key ideas in a way the intended audience can understand.

FAQs

Q: Can I present on a topic that is not directly related to the course content?

A: Yes, as long as you can justify the relevance of your topic to the course concepts.

Q: Can I work with a partner or in a group for this presentation?

A: Not recommended, but you may work in a group of no more than two people. Each member must contribute equally and demonstrate their own understanding of the topic.

Q: What are some recommended formats for the presentation?

A: You may deliver your presentation using slides, a poster, a live demonstration, or another format of your choice. The key is to effectively communicate your chosen topic and engage your audience.

Q: When will the presentations be scheduled?

A: Presentations may be scheduled during any regular class meeting at least two weeks before the final presentation week. We will coordinate scheduling in class to ensure everyone has an opportunity to present.

Q: How long should the presentation be?

A: The presentation should be approximately 5-10 minutes long, followed by a brief Q&A session. This allows you to provide a comprehensive overview of your topic while also engaging with your audience.
